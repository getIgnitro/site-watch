How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

By the KN Services team · 30 October 2026 · 4 minute read

The short answer: a small business website costs anywhere from a few hundred dollars to $15,000, depending on who builds it.

  • Do it yourself on a website builder: $0 to $800 to start, then about $10 to $120 a month.
  • Hire a freelancer: about $500 to $7,600 for the build.
  • Hire an agency: about $5,000 to $15,000 for a complete small business site.

After launch, budget for a domain, hosting and someone to keep the site updated. The rest of this guide shows where those numbers come from and what pushes a price up or down.

Three ways to get a website, side by side

Most small businesses choose one of three routes. The price gap between them is mostly a trade between your money and your time.

What pushes the price up

Two websites that look alike can be priced thousands of dollars apart. These are the things that usually make the difference:

  • Number of pages. A five-page site (home, about, services, gallery, contact) is a very different job from a 20-page site with a page for every service and every town you cover.
  • Booking, ordering or payments. Letting customers book an appointment, order ahead or pay online adds setup and testing time.
  • An online store. Product pages, a cart, checkout, shipping and returns are the biggest single jump in price. Larger agencies quote $15,000 to $60,000 or more for feature-rich and e-commerce sites.
  • Who writes the words and supplies the photos. If the builder writes your copy and sources images, expect to pay for it.
  • Custom design. A layout designed from scratch costs more than one adapted from a proven template.

The costs after launch

The build is only part of the bill. Every website also needs:

  • A domain name: a standard .com usually costs $12 to $25 a year.
  • Hosting: shared hosting plans run about $40 to $150 a year. Website builders include hosting in their monthly plan.
  • Upkeep: software updates, backups, security and small text or photo changes. This ranges from doing it yourself for free to a monthly fee paid to a freelancer or agency.

When you compare quotes, add up the first three years, not just the first invoice. A cheap build with an expensive monthly retainer can cost more than a higher one-off price.

What a fair quote should include

Before you sign anything, check that the quote answers these questions in writing:

  1. Which pages are included, listed by name?
  2. Who writes the text and who supplies the photos?
  3. Is the domain registered in your name?
  4. Are hosting, backups and security included, and for how long?
  5. Who makes small updates after launch, and is there a limit?
  6. What does the yearly cost become after the first year?
  7. If you leave, do you keep the website files and the domain?

A builder who answers all seven up front is easier to trust, and much easier to compare against the next quote.

Pay once or pay monthly

Builders charge in two ways. Some take one payment for the build and a smaller yearly or monthly fee for upkeep. Others charge a flat monthly fee with nothing upfront, which spreads the cost but usually totals more over time. Neither is wrong. Pay once is normally cheaper overall, and pay monthly is easier on cash flow when you are starting out.
